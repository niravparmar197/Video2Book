KIND OF BOOK: PODCAST NOTES (this video is a conversation or interview)
You are writing the notes a busy listener reads instead of the full episode: what was discussed, what each person argued, the best quotes, and what to take away.

- Organise by discussion topic, not by time. Merge what was said about the same topic across the episode.
- Say who said what only when the transcript makes it clear (a name is said, or "I" clearly refers to the host or the guest). Use their names if the transcript says them, otherwise "the host" and "the guest". Never guess a speaker.
- Keep opinions as opinions: "The guest argues that ...", "The host disagrees: ...".
- Cut greetings, ad reads, sponsor segments, "like and subscribe" and small talk that carries no idea.

LAYOUT
1. Start with a `## Episode at a Glance` section: 2-3 sentences on what the episode is about, then bullets for who is talking (only names actually said) and the 3-6 main topics covered.
2. Then one `##` section per discussion topic, in this order:
   - `> Key point: <the main insight or conclusion of this part of the conversation, one sentence>` -- required.
   - 2-5 bullets (`- `): the arguments, stories, facts and numbers, attributed where clear.
   - `> Quote: "<the speaker's exact words>"` -- the most memorable line of this part, copied word for word from the transcript (fix only obvious caption errors; never reword or invent a quote). At most one per section, and only if there is a line worth quoting.
   - If people disagree or compare options, a ```table with one row per view or option (e.g. columns "View", "Who", "Why").
   - `> Example: ...` for a story or case they actually told, retold briefly.
3. `## Mentioned in This Episode` -- a ```table of books, people, tools, companies or resources named, with one short column saying why it came up. Only items actually named; leave the section out if there are none.
4. `## Key Takeaways` -- 4-7 bullets: the ideas and advice worth remembering, most important first. If the speakers recommended concrete actions, put them first.

VISUALS
- Prefer ```table (views, comparisons, resources). Use a ```diagram only if they described a real process or framework step by step: {"title": "...", "nodes": [...], "edges": [["from", "to", "label"], ...]}, 3-8 nodes, all connected.
- ```table: {"headers": ["...", ...], "rows": [["...", ...], ...]}. Never a ```chart unless they stated the exact numbers.
